Regulatory Disclosure

Dining & Allergen Disclaimer

Last updated: September 10, 2026 | Effective immediately across supperbanquet.com

1. General Educational Purpose of Culinary and Nutritional Content

The culinary essays, sourdough fermentation guides, hearth cooking thermodynamics articles, and botanical beverage treatises published across supperbanquet.com are curated solely for general educational, cultural, and gastronomic appreciation. While our editorial contributors and chefs consult professional culinary literature and agrarian research, nothing published on this website constitutes formal nutritional counsel, medical dietary therapy, or clinical health advice.

Individuals with specific metabolic disorders, severe gastrointestinal conditions, or complex medical dietary restrictions should always consult a licensed physician, clinical dietitian, or certified medical nutritionist before adopting new dietary regimens. The recipes and culinary perspectives shared in our Gastronomy Journal celebrate traditional food preparation methods, but should never be substituted for individualized medical dietary guidance under real-world clinical circumstances.

2. Nature of Communal Dining and Allergen Cross-Contact Realities

SupperBanquet operates an artisanal scratch kitchen centered around a live-fire wood hearth, stone grain milling equipment, and family-style communal dining tables. Although our culinary team takes meticulous care to accommodate disclosed allergies and prepares parallel dishes for gluten-sensitive or dairy-free guests, our open kitchen handles flour, tree nuts, eggs, sesame, and shellfish continuously throughout daily service operations.

We do not operate a certified sterile gluten-free or allergen-free commercial kitchen facility. Because food is passed family-style among dinner guests across wooden tables, microscopic cross-contact may theoretically occur beyond the control of our kitchen brigade. Guests with life-threatening airborne or trace-contact food allergies must assess these operational realities and participate at their own calculated discretion.

3. Seasonal Harvest Variations and Agrarian Menu Adaptations

Our menus are dictated by the natural rhythms, weather conditions, and seasonal yields of our partner regenerative farms across New York and New England. Consequently, published online sample menus represent conceptual tasting journeys rather than rigid, unalterable culinary contracts, and specific vegetable cultivars, fish catches, or fruit varieties may be substituted on the day of service based on morning harvest availability.

A sudden early frost, prolonged drought, or maritime sea squall can alter crop availability with short notice, prompting our executive chef to adjust specific preparations to ensure only pristine, peak-flavor ingredients reach the banquet table. SupperBanquet reserves the culinary right to introduce fresh seasonal substitutions without prior digital notice, ensuring every dish honors our strict farm-to-table standards.

4. Zero-Proof Botanical Beverages and Non-Alcoholic Formulations

All house-crafted beverages, cold-pressed shrubs, botanical tonics, and fermented elixirs served at SupperBanquet are strictly non-alcoholic (0.0% ABV). Our pairings are designed to celebrate the natural complexity of whole fruit acids, herbal tannins, bitter roots, and floral aromatics, providing an elevated culinary pairing experience that maintains total sensory clarity throughout the evening.

While our botanical tonics utilize natural herbs, roots, and botanicals celebrated in traditional gastronomy, they are formulated as culinary refreshments rather than medicinal herbal remedies, therapeutic supplements, or pharmaceutical preparations. Individuals who are pregnant, nursing, or taking specific prescription medications that interact with botanicals (such as concentrated grapefruit oils or bitter gentian roots) should review pairing ingredients with our staff.

5. External Citations, Hyperlinks, and Independent Partner Limits

Our website and journal publications may occasionally reference independent heirloom seed conservancies, agricultural universities, artisanal ceramicists, or slow food associations to provide historical context for our dining philosophy. These citations, hyperlinks, and editorial mentions are offered solely as an informational courtesy to our readers and do not imply an endorsement, formal sponsorship, commercial partnership, or shared legal liability with external organizations.

SupperBanquet exercises no editorial control over external websites, third-party content policies, or external commercial practices, and assumes zero legal responsibility for the accuracy or integrity of information found on third-party sites linked from our pages. Patrons navigating to third-party web domains do so entirely at their own discretion and are subject to the independent terms and privacy protocols of those respective external entities.
